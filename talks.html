2026

23rd National Space Science Symposium (NSSS 2026)

23–27 February 2026 · North Eastern Space Applications Centre (NESAC), Meghalaya
Organised by North Eastern Space Applications Centre (NESAC) and Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO)
Poster Presentation

My Contribution

At NSSS 2026, I presented a poster titled “An Integrated Framework for Modeling Daytime and Seasonal Variability in VLF Signal Propagation” . The work presented an integrated numerical framework combining the D-region electron continuity equation (ECE), Wait’s ionospheric model and the LWPC propagation code to simulate sub-ionospheric VLF signal propagation.

The framework was applied to the 18.2 kHz VTX transmitter and validated using multi-station VLF observations from the ICSP summer and winter campaigns conducted during solar-minimum conditions. The study reproduced the temporal behaviour of the observed VLF signals and was further used to investigate their spatial distribution over India.

A particular focus of the work was the prominent east–west asymmetry observed in VLF propagation. Spatial simulations were performed at different times of the day to examine the evolution of the VLF signal propagation pattern during the summer and winter seasons.

2025

3rd Young Astronomers of Today's India (YATI3)

7–9 October 2025 · Indian Centre for Space Physics (ICSP), Kolkata
Oral Presentation

My Contribution

At YATI3, I presented an overview of my doctoral research on the modelling of D-region ionospheric responses to solar forcing and their manifestation in sub-ionospheric VLF radio signals. The presentation covered the physical connection between solar X-ray and EUV radiation and the lower ionosphere, with particular emphasis on the D-region, which acts as the primary reflecting region for sub-ionospheric VLF propagation.

A major part of the presentation focused on an integrated autonomous modelling framework in which the D-region electron continuity equation is solved to obtain the temporal and altitude-dependent electron density profile. The resulting electron density profiles are then used to extract Wait's empirical ionospheric reflection parameters through a log-linear method, which are subsequently supplied to the LWPC framework to simulate VLF signal amplitudes. The model was validated against observations from ICSP's VLF receiving stations. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

The solar-flare component of the work considered several C-class solar flares and investigated their effects along multiple VLF propagation paths, including VTX–IERCOO, GQD–NTZ and NSY–NTZ. The presentation demonstrated how the model can reproduce the observed VLF signal perturbations associated with enhanced solar X-ray irradiance. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2} :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

I also presented an extension of the framework to quiet-time conditions, where solar EUV radiation and the diurnal variation of the ionosphere become important. The model was applied to multi-station summer and winter VLF campaigns conducted by ICSP during 2008–09, demonstrating its applicability beyond transient solar-flare events. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

The overall objective was to demonstrate that a unified modelling framework can connect solar irradiance, D-region electron density, ionospheric reflection parameters and VLF propagation, providing a physically motivated interpretation of observed VLF signal variations under both disturbed and quiet solar conditions. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}
