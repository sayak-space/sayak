About Me

Researcher in ionospheric physics and solar-terrestrial science

Sayak Chakraborty

I am a space physics researcher working at the intersection of ionospheric physics, solar-terrestrial interactions and radio-wave remote sensing. My research primarily focuses on understanding the response of the lower ionosphere to solar and geophysical forcing, with particular emphasis on the D-region ionosphere and sub-ionospheric Very Low Frequency (VLF) radio-wave propagation.

My research combines theoretical modelling, numerical simulations and observational analysis to investigate the dynamics of the lower ionosphere during transient solar events. A major part of my doctoral research involved studying the temporal and altitude-dependent response of D-region electron density to solar ionization and understanding how these changes manifest themselves in VLF radio signals.

I am particularly interested in developing physically motivated models that can connect solar forcing, ionospheric response and measurable radio-wave perturbations.

Research Journey

My research journey in ionospheric science began during my M.Sc. project, where I investigated the theoretical behaviour of electron density and response time delay in the mid-latitudinal lower ionosphere during solar flares. This work introduced me to the physics of the D-region and to the use of numerical modelling for studying solar-driven ionospheric disturbances.

During my doctoral research at the Indian Centre for Space Physics, I developed this work further by investigating the response of the lower ionosphere to solar ionization using electron continuity-based modelling. An important aspect of this research was understanding how the response varies with altitude and how quickly different parts of the D-region react to changes in solar radiation.

I subsequently extended the modelling towards sub-ionospheric VLF radio-wave propagation. VLF signals provide a valuable means of remotely sensing the lower ionosphere because their propagation characteristics are strongly influenced by the electron density structure of the D-region.

This led to a combined approach involving electron density modelling, ionospheric parameter extraction and VLF propagation simulations. More recently, my research has expanded towards multi-path VLF observations, solar eclipses and the response of the lower ionosphere to relatively weak solar flares.

At ARIES, I am now extending this research direction towards observations of the Sun and their implications for the coupled Sun–Earth system, with particular interest in making connections between solar activity, atmospheric response and space-weather-related phenomena.

Solar Flare Responses

Ionospheric disturbances and response-time characteristics associated with solar X-ray and EUV enhancements.

Solar–Terrestrial Physics

Physical connections between solar activity, Earth's ionosphere and the near-Earth environment.

Space Weather

Understanding how solar variability propagates through the Sun–Earth system and influences geospace.

Ionospheric Modelling

Numerical and theoretical modelling of solar-driven changes in ionospheric electron density and radio-wave propagation.
